# Sourcing & disclosure (Day 50)
**Paper-sourced** (abstract via arxiv.org/abs/2610.28184, fetched 2026-10-24; full text not read): title, authors (Cercola, Luo, Formentin, Pavone), submission 2026-10-20; finding that hazards are encoded but plans track ego speed history ("perception-action binding gap"); training-free hazard-conditioned energy added to the frozen flow-matching sampler of Alpamayo-1.5; with exact obstacle geometry: footprint intrusion 58.1% -> 14.5%, collisions eliminated in braking-relevant closed-loop tests.
**Everything else is this repo's own reconstruction**: the tiny MLP flow-matching net (327k params), synthetic 1-D-braking scenes, the SDF-based energy, the guidance schedule `lambda*(0.25+t)`, lateral-gradient damping (0.05), the guidance trust-region cap (2.0x velocity norm), margin 5.0 m, receding-horizon closed loop, and all numbers in `assets/results.json`.
**Not paper results.** The similar-looking 47%->0.5% intrusion drop is a synthetic result on a model deliberately trained with rare (8%) hazard demos, so a binding gap exists by construction.
**Honest findings:** (1) my "binding-gap" sensitivity probe did NOT reproduce the paper's pattern: the baseline plan length responds to hazard distance at least as strongly as to speed (ratio 1.9), it just under-brakes. (2) Naive guidance (lambda=40, no cap, small margin) diverged (backward/lateral plans); fixed with lateral damping + trust-region cap. (3) The guidance margin is a safety/progress trade-off: margin 0.8 m left 40-87% closed-loop collisions; 5 m gives 1.7% with no progress loss for far hazards. (4) `simulate.py` shows a *selected* scene where baseline collides and guided does not.
